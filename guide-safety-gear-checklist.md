## Boat Safety Gear Checklist: What Expires and When

Most boats carry the right safety gear on the day they are bought. The trouble starts later, when a flare passes its date, an extinguisher gauge drops, or a life jacket goes soft at the seams and nobody notices.

Use this checklist at the start of the season, and again before any long trip.

### Important first: requirements vary

What you must carry depends on your boat's length, type, where you boat and your state's rules. Federal rules from the U.S. Coast Guard set the baseline, and states can add to them. Check the current rules for your boat before you rely on any list, including this one.

### The checklist

**Life jackets (PFDs)**
- One wearable jacket for every person aboard, in the right size
- Easy to reach, not buried in a locker
- Look for tears, mildew, broken buckles and faded fabric
- Inflatable types: check the cylinder and indicator, and follow the maker's service schedule
- A throwable device (ring or cushion) where it is required for your boat

**Fire extinguishers**
- Right type and number for your boat's length and engine setup
- Gauge in the green, pin and seal intact, no dents or corrosion
- Mounted where you can reach it, not inside the engine space
- Check the label or the maker for the replacement or service date

**Visual distress signals (flares)**
- Signals suited to where you boat, in date and stored dry
- Pyrotechnic flares carry an expiration date on the package, which is typically about 42 months from manufacture. Read your own label.
- Dispose of expired flares properly, and keep them only as extra backups if allowed locally

**Sound signals**
- Horn or whistle that works and can be heard
- Test it every trip

**Navigation lights**
- Check each light before a night trip and replace bulbs
- Spare bulbs or fuses aboard

**Communication**
- VHF radio working, with a handheld backup
- Phones charged, in a waterproof case
- EPIRB or personal locator beacon, if you carry one: check the battery date and registration

**Other items**
- First aid kit, restocked, with medicines in date
- Anchor and enough line
- Bailer or bilge pump that works
- Fire-safe fuel setup: check hoses, clamps and the vent
- Flashlight with fresh batteries
- Copies of your registration and insurance

### Which items expire

| Item | What to check | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| Pyrotechnic flares | Date printed on each flare | About 42 months from manufacture |
| Fire extinguisher | Gauge, label, service tag | Per label and maker |
| Inflatable life jacket | Cylinder, bobbin, indicator | Per maker |
| EPIRB or PLB | Battery date, registration | Per maker |
| First aid kit | Medicine and bandage dates | Check yearly |
| Batteries and bulbs | Condition and charge | Check each trip |

### Write the dates down

The easiest way to get caught out is to keep the dates in your head. Write each item, its expiration or service date and where it is stored. Set a reminder a month before each date so you have time to replace it.
