## How to File a Float Plan

A float plan is a short written note that tells someone on shore where you are going, who is aboard and when you expect to be back. If something goes wrong, it gives rescuers a place to start looking instead of a whole coastline.

You do not file it with the Coast Guard. You leave it with a person you trust, or with a marina, and you agree on what they should do if you are late.

### Who should hold your float plan

Pick someone who will notice if you do not come back and will act on it:

- A family member or friend who is reachable on the day
- The marina or yacht club office
- A shore-side crew member who is not on the trip

Tell them in plain words: "If I have not called by 6 p.m., phone me. If you cannot reach me by 7 p.m., call the Coast Guard or local marine police."

### What to put in a float plan

**About the boat**
- Name, registration or documentation number
- Type, length, hull color and any distinctive marks
- Engines and fuel type, and how much fuel is aboard
- Home port and where it is kept

**About the people**
- Names and phone numbers of everyone aboard
- Any medical conditions or medication that matter in an emergency
- Name and number of the person holding the plan

**About the trip**
- Departure point and time
- Route and planned stops
- Destination and expected return time
- A "call me by" time, plus a later "raise the alarm" time

**About your gear**
- Radios (VHF channel you will monitor, handheld or fixed)
- Phone numbers aboard, and whether there is signal along the route
- Life jackets, flares, horn, extinguisher, first aid kit, anchor
- EPIRB or personal locator beacon, if you carry one

### Checklist before you leave

- Plan written down and handed over
- Everyone aboard listed
- "Call me by" and "raise the alarm" times agreed
- Weather and tide checked
- Fuel enough for the trip plus a reserve
- Phones and handheld radio charged

### If your plans change

Call or message your contact when you change your route, your destination or your timing. A plan that no longer matches reality is worse than none, because people will search the wrong place.

### Close it out when you are back

The most important step is the last one. As soon as you are tied up, tell your contact you are safe. If you skip this, someone may call for a rescue you do not need.
